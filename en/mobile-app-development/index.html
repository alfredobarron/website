When an app is worth it

An app is worth the investment when your customers or your team will use it often, and from their phones. It makes sense when:

  • You need to reach the user right away, with notifications.
  • The work happens on the street, in a warehouse or at a gatehouse, away from a computer.
  • It uses the camera, a code scanner or the location.
  • Your customers repeat the same task several times a month.

If it will only be used now and then, a good website that works on the phone is almost always enough, and I tell you so before quoting.

The first version of an app usually takes two to four months, including the store reviews.

Frequently asked questions

What technology do you build apps with?

React Native and Expo. A single codebase produces the iOS app and the Android app, and both use the native components of each system.

Do you publish the app on the App Store and Google Play?

Yes. I handle the whole process: the developer accounts, the listing, the screenshots, the privacy policy and the replies to the Apple and Google reviews.

Does an app also need a web system?

Almost always. The app is what your customer or your staff uses on the phone; behind it there is a server that stores the information and a web dashboard you manage it from. I build all three.

Does the app work offline?

It can be designed for that when the job requires it. In Privada, for example, the gatehouse keeps validating access when the connection drops and syncs when it comes back.
